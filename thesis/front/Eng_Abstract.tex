Field-deployable turbulent-flow reconstruction must infer a continuous field from sparse velocity sensors ($K \sim 10^2$) under low-latency inference and without Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) full-field supervision. Existing reduced-order, supervised, and physics-informed approaches either rely on offline full-field trajectories, assume dense inputs, or require solver-like retraining for each instance. The main challenge is to turn a sparse sensor stream into a queryable field while enforcing the Navier--Stokes (NS) equations and preserving deployment-compatible supervision. This work introduces \textbf{PI-CON} (\textbf{P}hysics-\textbf{I}nformed \textbf{C}ontinuous-time \textbf{O}perator \textbf{N}etwork), a Deep Operator Network (DeepONet) with a Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) sensor encoder and a distance-biased cross-attention readout. PI-CON is trained only on sensor measurements and NS residuals, and the study quantifies how sensor count, placement, and noise govern reconstruction quality.

On the 2D Kolmogorov flow at $Re=10\,000$ with $K=100$ sensors, PI-CON reconstructs within the engineering target under sensor-plus-physics supervision: over five random seeds it attains a Kinetic Energy (KE) relative error of $\mathbf{5.71 \pm 0.11\%}$, with the energy-dominant low-wavenumber band within single-digit error. Once trained, it reconstructs any query point in a single forward pass without re-solving the flow. The same architecture extends to $Re=10^6$ ($6.10\%$ KE error at $K=200$).

The sensing configuration sets resolution and reliability, not feasibility. The mid-high-wavenumber band ($k > k_{\max}^{\rm sensor}\approx 5.64$) is limited by the $K=100$ sensor information, not by the architecture: $\sqrt{K/\pi}$ is a Nyquist-like scale, and a $K$-scaling sweep ($K \in \{100, 200, 400\}$) confirms the cutoff moves accordingly. Sensor values come from a DNS field standing in for the inaccessible real flow, but the full DNS field never supervises training. Across DNS-pivot, DNS-free Large-Eddy-Simulation (LES), and random placement, reconstruction remains within the engineering target; placement variance is about six times training stochasticity, and additive sensor noise up to $10\%$ shifts KE by under one percentage point.
