\subsection{Forcing-parameter recovery fails under the tested setup}
\label{sec:forcing_identifiability}

The Kolmogorov forcing has two free parameters, amplitude $A$ and wavenumber $k_f$, held at $A=0.1$, $k_f=2$ in the main pipeline. Three configurations test whether the architecture can recover them from sensor data alone, with near-zero initialisation ($A_{\rm init}=0.001$, $k_{f,\rm init}=0.01$) in the flat region of the sigmoid/log-$A$ parameterisation. All three start from this single flat-region initialisation, so the diagnostic probes recovery under the tested setup rather than structural identifiability.

\paragraph{Identifiability verdict.}
Learned forcing parameters drift two orders of magnitude from truth, yet KE reconstruction remains close to the hardcoded reference: the forcing-mode contribution to $u, v$ is small relative to advection and diffusion, so reconstruction is insensitive to forcing accuracy at this sensor budget. The reverse implication is stronger: at $K=100$, accurate reconstruction does not certify forcing recovery. Whether the forcing is structurally unidentifiable, as opposed to unrecovered under the tested initialisation, optimiser, and parameterisation, is not established here; recovering it likely requires a larger budget, multiple initialisations, or an explicit spectral-peak prior.

\section{Engineering-Transferable Interpolation Baselines}
\label{app:fair_baselines}

Three classical reconstructions respect the same sensor-only constraint as PI-CON (no DNS access at training or inference): radial basis function (RBF) interpolation with a multiquadric kernel, inverse distance weighting (IDW), and a divergence-free trigonometric least-squares fit on a truncated Fourier basis ($k_{\max}=5$, matching the $K=100$ Nyquist cutoff). Table~\ref{tab:fair_baselines} compares them with PI-CON on the same $K=100$ LES-derived sensors used for the main baseline, so the comparison isolates the reconstruction method at a fixed sensor placement. The RBF shape parameter ($\varepsilon=10$, of order one sensor spacing) and the truncation ($k_{\max}=5$, the $K=100$ Nyquist scale) are fixed from these a-priori scales rather than tuned to minimise DNS error, keeping the baselines sensor-only.

PI-CON: mean $\pm$ standard deviation over $n=5$ seeds. Rightmost column: PI-CON relative reduction in $u$ rel-$L_2$. Relative-error metrics are percentages.}
    \label{tab:fair_baselines}

    \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}

\paragraph{Kinetic energy is a misleading sole metric.}
RBF multiquadric and the divergence-free trigonometric least-squares post lower KE error ($5.08\%$ and $4.42\%$) than the PI-CON multi-seed mean ($5.71\%$), yet both reconstruct the field markedly worse pointwise. Classical interpolation contracts the velocity toward the inter-sensor mean, suppressing total kinetic energy and collapsing the KE ratio toward the DNS value for the wrong reason; the pointwise $u$- and $v$-$L_2$ rows expose this. PI-CON reduces $u$ rel-$L_2$ by $47.2\%$ relative to the strongest fair baseline (trigonometric LSQ, $25.87\%\to13.65\%$) and by $74.2\%$ relative to IDW. Pointwise error, not KE alone, separates reconstruction from energy contraction.

The refreshed interpolation sweep also shows strong hyperparameter sensitivity: under the same sensors, over-complete trigonometric LSQ at $k_{\max}=8$ and $12$ and poorly chosen RBF length scales can produce errors orders of magnitude larger than the carefully selected rows in Table~\ref{tab:fair_baselines}. These unstable variants are excluded from the main comparison table and treated as evidence that classical interpolation baselines require explicit validation rather than default trust.

\section{Open-Loop Forward-CFD Baseline}
\label{sec:forward_cfd_baseline}

A natural CFD-side reference reconstructs a divergence-free initial field from the $K=100$ sensor measurements at $t=0$, then forward-integrates the pseudo-spectral ETDRK4 solver used for the reference DNS. Both the Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) reconstruction and the forward integration run on the stored $256^2$ snapshot grid (Table~\ref{tab:dns_params}); only the scoring reference remains the $1024^2$ DNS downsampled to $256^2$. Because the field is reconstructed from the initial-time sensors alone and then integrated freely, with no later sensor data assimilated, this is an open-loop forecast reference rather than a matched assimilation baseline: PI-CON, by contrast, ingests the full sensor time series. The comparison therefore contrasts open-loop forecasting with continuous assimilation, isolating what a purely physics-driven forecast attains from the same initial-time sensor budget without learned components.

\paragraph{Construction.}
Leading-rank $r=40$ POD modes are computed from $200$ DNS snapshots over $t\in[0,5]$ jointly over the $u$ and $v$ channels; the modes are divergence-free by construction. Sensor measurements at $t=0$ are projected onto these modes by least squares,
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\;\big\Vert \mathbf{y}_{\rm sensor} - \boldsymbol\Phi_{\rm at\,sensor}\boldsymbol\alpha \big\Vert_2^2,\qquad
    \hat{\mathbf{u}}_0(\mathbf{x}) = \bar{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x}) + \sum_{r}\alpha_r\,\boldsymbol\Phi_r(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}
giving a divergence-free initial field that interpolates the $K=100$ sensors. The reconstructed $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_0$ is advanced to $t=5$ with the same fp64 ETDRK4 solver, dealiasing rule, time step ($\Delta t = 2.5\times10^{-4}$), and forcing as the reference DNS. The $t=0$ projection is near-exact in KE (rel-err $0.08\%$); residual pointwise $u$-$L_2$ ($5.21\%$) and $v$-$L_2$ ($6.16\%$) reflect energy outside the leading-$40$ subspace.

\paragraph{Same-attractor diagnosis.}
The Forward-CFD baseline appears KE-competitive, while pointwise metrics reveal phase decorrelation. Three observations isolate the cause.

\emph{Bounded statistics are preserved.} At $t=5$ the baseline gives $\mathrm{KE}=0.1200$ vs.\ DNS $0.1248$ ($-3.85\%$) and enstrophy $\Omega=14.65$ vs.\ $14.16$ ($+3.46\%$); the dissipation balance is intact.

\emph{The energy spectrum matches DNS within $\approx 10\%$ for $k\in[1,120]$} (Fig.~\ref{fig:forward_cfd_spectrum}), sharing the $k^{-3}$ enstrophy-cascade slope and dissipation roll-off; both fields lie on the same invariant measure of the chaotic attractor.

\emph{The forcing-induced anisotropy preserved by DNS at $t=5$} ($\sigma_u/\sigma_v=2.32$) is lost in the baseline ($0.90$, with $\sigma_v$ exceeding $\sigma_u$; Fig.~\ref{fig:forward_cfd_spectrum}, right). Forward integration over $T/t_{\rm eddy}=2.51$ eddy-turnover times (\S\ref{subsec:dns_verification}) loses the directional bias injected by $f_x=A\sin(2\pi k_f y)$ once IC error grows.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Forward integration from a sensor-reconstructed initial condition cannot recover pointwise phase at $t=5$: over the five-second horizon ($\approx 2.5$ eddy-turnover times) chaotic growth of the initial reconstruction error drives $u$-$L_2$ to $152.8\%$, by which point pointwise phase is lost. KE alone misranks the comparison: the baseline sits on the attractor's invariant measure but at a phase fully decorrelated from the DNS realisation, whereas PI-CON re-conditions on the sensor stream throughout $t\in[0,5]$ and tracks the realisation. The pointwise gap at the matched final time is an order of magnitude ($u$-$L_2$ $152.8\% \to 7.28\%$ is $21\times$; $v$-$L_2$ $203.9\% \to 16.38\%$ is $12.4\times$), reflecting the structural advantage of continuous re-conditioning. The baseline has no recovery path within its own framework: a denser POD rank would not stabilise the phase, since the leading modes already capture $>99\%$ of the energy. Reducing the pointwise error requires either a continuous sensor stream (the PI-CON regime) or a 4D-variational assimilation loop on top of the forward solver.
